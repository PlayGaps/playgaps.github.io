1. Who This Policy Covers

This Kids Privacy Policy applies to every educational game published under the Play Gaps name on Google Play — our full library of 16 games today, including Times Tables for Beginners and Color Peek, plus any future Play Gaps game built for children. Where one game works differently, it has its own section below (sections 8 and 9). It is written specifically to meet Google Play's Designed for Families requirements and the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA), including the amendments that reached full compliance in April 2026.

If Play Gaps ever publishes an app meant for a general, non-child audience, that app will link to a separate privacy policy written for that audience instead of this one.

2. Our Approach, in One Sentence

Every Play Gaps game is built so a child can play, learn, and have fun without ever being asked for — or needing to hand over — any personal information at all.

3. Information We Do Not Collect

There is no sign-up and no login in any Play Gaps game. Specifically, we do not collect, request, or store on our side:

  • Your child's name, nickname, email address, or phone number
  • Photos, videos, voice recordings, or any biometric data (fingerprints, or face or voice patterns)
  • The phone's precise location. Google's ad software can tell a rough area, such as a city, from the phone's internet address (section 4).
  • Contacts, files, or anything else from the device
  • Any account, password, or persistent user profile

Every game is designed to be picked up and played immediately — no form to fill in, nothing to create an account for. The one exception to typing: Times Tables for Beginners lets a parent type a child's first name so the game can say "Well done, Mia!". It is optional, it stays on your phone, and we never receive it (section 8).

4. Information Collected Automatically

Apart from the Google Play and purchase services named in section 7, the main way any technical information is processed is through Google AdMob, which we use to show ads and keep our games free. AdMob's own software, running inside the game, may automatically process a small amount of technical data on Google's servers — for example, general device type, an app identifier and IP address (the phone's internet address, from which Google can tell a rough area such as a city, never an exact place) — purely to serve an ad, detect fraud, and prevent the same ad from repeating too often.

We rely on COPPA's "support for internal operations" exception for this: the data is used only for those internal purposes, never to build a profile of your child, track them across other apps or websites, or contact them individually. Because every Play Gaps game is configured for child-directed advertising, current Google Play policy also blocks the advertising identifier itself from being sent along with these ad requests.

5. Advertising in Our Games

Every Play Gaps game is tagged as child-directed, and every ad request is capped at Google's "G" (General Audiences) content rating. In practice, that means:

  • Ads are contextual and non-personalized — never chosen based on a child's behavior, interests, or profile
  • We never show remarketing or behavioral advertising to children
  • We only use ad partners certified for Google Play's Families program

You can read how Google itself handles this limited advertising data in Google's Ads Policy and Google's Privacy Policy.

6. In-App Purchases

Some Play Gaps games offer optional in-app purchases — for example, removing ads. Every purchase is handled entirely by Google Play's own billing system. In Color Peek, Unity's purchase software runs inside the game, and payments are made through Google Play (section 9). Play Gaps never receives, sees, or stores any payment card, bank, or financial information — that part of the transaction happens entirely within Google Play, governed by Google's own policies. Google Play requires you to confirm each purchase with your account password, PIN, or device biometrics, which helps ensure purchases are completed by a parent, not a child.

7. Third-Party Services We Use

We keep this list short on purpose. Every Play Gaps game uses:

  • Google AdMob (advertising) and Google Play Billing (in-app purchases) — both part of Google. See Google's Privacy Policy.

Times Tables for Beginners also uses three more Google services, for the reasons in section 8:

  • Google's consent tool (the User Messaging Platform), which the game asks what the law allows before any ad loads.
  • Google Play's in-app updates, so the game can offer a newer version.
  • Google Play's in-app review, so a grown-up can rate the game.

Color Peek also uses three more services, for the reasons in section 9:

  • Google's consent tool (the User Messaging Platform), which the game asks what the law allows before any ad loads.
  • Unity In-App Purchasing, purchase software from Unity Technologies that runs inside the game and connects it to Google Play Billing for its one purchase; the payment is made through Google Play. Unity's documentation lists only basic technical information about how it is working, never personal information, and it is not shared (section 9).
  • The Play Store app: Rate opens the game's page there, so a grown-up can rate it.

Times Tables Chart also uses these services:

  • Google's consent tool (the User Messaging Platform), which the game asks what the law allows before any ad loads.
  • Google Play's in-app updates, so the game can offer a newer version. The game asks Google Play whether a newer version is out; Google Play handles this under Google's own privacy policy.
  • Google Play's in-app review, so a grown-up can rate the game.
  • Unity In-App Purchasing, purchase software from Unity Technologies that runs inside the game and connects it to Google Play Billing for its one purchase, "Remove Ads"; the payment is made through Google Play, and Google Play's billing library sends Google its own technical logs. Unity's documentation lists only basic technical information about how the purchase software is working, never personal information, and it is not shared. The game never starts the Unity online services that would send it.
  • An internet check. When the game opens or comes back on screen, it briefly opens www.google.com (or www.cloudflare.com if that fails) to see whether the phone is online, and checks again every few seconds while it is offline. Like any visit to a web page, this shows those companies the phone's internet address; nothing else is sent.

We do not use Firebase, Unity Analytics, Google Play Games Services, social login, or any other analytics, tracking, or advertising SDK in any Play Gaps game.

8. Times Tables for Beginners

Everything above applies to Times Tables for Beginners. This section adds what is different in this one game.

What the game keeps on your phone. So your child can carry on where they left off, the game keeps, on the phone only:

  • your child's first name, only if you type one (you can leave it empty);
  • answers, stars, coins, the path through the tables, outfits and places bought with coins, and which certificates were won;
  • minutes practised each day, shown to you in "Grown-ups" (the lock at the top of the home screen);
  • settings: sound, Tally's voice, vibration, light or dark, the school's way of writing tables, the daily reminder's time, and whether the Sunday note is on;
  • for the Sunday note: the day each table was first met, how many notes in a row nobody opened (it stops after three) and the day the last one came. The note itself is shown by the phone, in its notifications, on Sundays if you turn it on: your child's name, the days and minutes played that week, the tables met and one tricky fact — so it can be seen on the phone's lock screen, as the daily reminder can;
  • small records the "Grown-ups" area uses so it does not ask twice or call too often: the day a grown-up last opened it and the tables met by then, whether it has been opened on this phone and the day the "New" mark on its door began; and, for rating the game, how many times a grown-up has said "Not now" to our request and the day they last did, whether and on which day a grown-up last went to the game's Play page, and how many times, and on which day, Google Play's rating window was last asked for.

None of this is sent to us or to anyone else by the game. A certificate leaves the phone only when you share or print it yourself.

Your phone's backup. If your phone's backup is on (Android 9 and later), Android copies your child's profile and progress into your own Google account, so they come back on a new phone. The game allows this only into a backup encrypted with your phone's screen lock, or straight from one phone to another. We cannot see these backups. On Android 7 and 8 nothing is copied.

Ads. Ads come from Google AdMob only, which is on Google Play's list of advertising services approved for apps for children. No other ad network is used.

  • No ad at the end of your child's first lesson. After that, an ad may come when a lesson or a game ends — only then, never during one, and never in the first minute after the game opens. There is always a whole lesson or game between two ads.
  • Every ad request says the user is a child, under the age of consent, and asks only for ads suitable for all ages (rated G). The game does not ask the phone for its advertising ID.
  • The game sends nothing to Google's advertising service until your child has finished their first lesson, and nothing more once Remove ads is bought.

While ads are possible, Google receives: the phone's internet address, which gives a rough area such as a city (never an exact place); how the game and the ad were used (the game opening, taps on an ad, an ad being watched); how the game and the ad software are working, including crashes; and device and account identifiers, such as Google's "app set ID" and, if a Google account is signed in on the phone, identifiers related to it (never the advertising ID). It is sent encrypted, and used only to show and count ads, limit how often one is shown, prevent fraud and keep the ads working.

How long Google keeps it. Google says it removes part of the internet address from its advertising logs after 9 months, and identifier information after 18 months (Google: how we retain data).

Google's consent tool (United Kingdom and Europe). Before any ad loads, the game asks Google's consent tool what the law allows. The game tells it every user is under the age of consent, so it never asks your child to agree to anything. If the law requires a way to change privacy choices for ads, "Privacy choices for ads" appears in Grown-ups → Settings.

Google Play. Remove ads is one payment through Google Play, not a subscription. The game also asks Google Play whether a newer version exists, and a grown-up can choose to rate the game there. Google Play handles these under Google's own privacy policy. Google Play's billing library inside the game also sends Google technical logs about its own work, even after Remove ads is bought: whether its calls to Google Play worked, the game's and the library's versions, the phone's model and settings (such as its language, country and kind of connection), and the phone's internet address. Google uses them to keep purchases working.

In "Grown-ups", after a grown-up has printed or shared a certificate, Google Play's own rating window may appear — at most three times, months apart. It is Google Play's, under Google's own privacy policy: the stars and any words typed in it go from the Play Store to Google, under the grown-up's own Google account, as a rating on the Play page would. The game never receives them, and cannot tell whether the window was shown.

Deleting everything. Remove the game from the phone, and everything it kept goes with it. Or, to keep the game for another child, use Grown-ups → Settings → "Start again for a new child". A copy in your phone's backup is in your own Google account; you can delete it in your phone's backup settings.

9. Color Peek

Everything above applies to Color Peek. This section adds what is different in this one game.

What the game keeps on your phone. So your child can carry on where they left off, the game keeps, on the phone only: the coins earned; which pictures are finished (the stickers), in the order they were finished, and which stickers your child has looked at in the book; the gold stars earned; how far the village has filled with colour; until when a 24-hour unlock of special pictures lasts; whether "Turn Ads Off" was bought; whether the game has been opened before; what the menu has already shown, so nothing repeats; and on which day the opening animation last played in full. There is nothing to type, and no sign-up or account. None of this is sent to us or to anyone else by the game.

Your phone's backup. If your phone's backup is on, Android may copy this progress into your own Google account, so it comes back on a new phone. We cannot see these backups.

Ads. Ads come from Google AdMob only, which is on Google Play's list of advertising services approved for apps for children. No other ad network is used.

  • Ads come only at breaks, never while your child is colouring. A full-screen ad may come when your child finishes a picture and taps Next, or just after going back to the menu. There are at least 5 minutes between two full-screen ads, and none in the first 5 minutes after the game opens. A small banner ad shows at the bottom of the screen only once a picture is finished, and goes away when your child moves on, starts the picture again or goes back.
  • There are no ads at all the first time the game is opened after it is installed.
  • Every ad request says the user is a child, under the age of consent, and asks only for non-personalised ads suitable for all ages (rated G). The game does not ask the phone for its advertising ID.
  • Once "Turn Ads Off" is bought, the game stops loading ads at once. The next times it opens, it no longer starts Google's ad software or Google's consent tool.

Until "Turn Ads Off" is bought, the game starts Google's ad software each time it opens, even the very first time, when no ad is shown. Google then receives: the phone's internet address, which gives a rough area such as a city (never an exact place); how the game and the ads were used (the game opening, taps on an ad, an ad being watched); how the game and the ad software are working, such as how long the game takes to open; and device and account identifiers, such as Google's "app set ID" and, if a Google account is signed in on the phone, identifiers related to it (never the advertising ID). It is sent encrypted, and used only to show and count ads, limit how often one is shown, prevent fraud and keep the ads working. Google says it removes part of the internet address from its advertising logs after 9 months, and identifier information after 18 months (Google: how we retain data).

Google's consent tool. Until "Turn Ads Off" is bought, each time the game opens, in every country, and before any ad loads, the game asks Google's consent tool what the law allows where the phone is. The game tells it every user is under the age of consent, so it never asks your child to agree to anything.

The one purchase, "Turn Ads Off". It is one payment through Google Play, not a subscription, and it sits in the Parents area behind a grown-up check. Google Play takes the payment, so we never see card or bank details. The game uses Unity In-App Purchasing, purchase software from Unity Technologies that runs inside the game. It asks Google Play for the price, passes on the purchase, and checks whether "Turn Ads Off" was bought; the game keeps the answer on the phone. Unity's documentation for this version lists only one kind of data it can collect: basic technical information about how the purchase software is working. It is never personal information, and it is not shared. Color Peek never starts the Unity online services that would send it.

Google Play's billing library. Until "Turn Ads Off" is bought (or restored) on this phone, Unity's purchase software connects to Google Play each time the game opens, to get the price and check for an earlier purchase. While it is connected, Google Play's billing library inside the game sends Google its own technical logs: whether its calls to Google Play worked, the game's and the library's versions, the phone's model and settings (such as its language, country and kind of connection), and the phone's internet address. Google uses them to keep purchases working. Once the purchase is recorded on the phone, the game no longer connects to Google Play's billing when it opens again.

Google Play. Rate opens the game's page in the Play Store app, where a grown-up can choose to rate it; Google Play handles that under Google's own privacy policy. Share sends only a ready-made message with the game's name and its Play Store link, never anything about your child or the phone. Rate, Share, our other games, the purchase and the link to this policy all sit behind the grown-up check.

Deleting everything. Remove the game from the phone, and everything it kept goes with it. A copy in your phone's backup is in your own Google account; you can delete it in your phone's backup settings. To ask for any data about your child to be deleted, email contact@playgaps.com. We keep no data about your child on any server, and we will help you ask Google about the data its ad software received.

Risks and how we reduce them. We looked at what could go wrong for a child in Color Peek, and at what makes each risk smaller. We review this at each new version.

  • Ads: an ad might not be right for a child. Every ad request says the user is a child and asks only for non-personalised ads rated G, and we block the sensitive topics on AdMob's standard list. Ads come only at breaks, never while your child is colouring. If you see an ad that is not right for children, tell us at contact@playgaps.com. Tapping an ad can open the Play Store or a website.
  • Data: there is no account, no name, nothing to type and no advertising ID. What remains: from the phone's internet address, Google's ad software can tell a rough area, such as a city, and may use it to choose an ad. It does not choose ads from what your child did before.
  • Purchases: a child might try to buy. The one purchase is behind a grown-up check (a simple sum). A child who can add, or who guesses, can pass it. Then Google Play asks the buyer to confirm the purchase, with the Google account password, the phone's PIN or fingerprint, or a Google prompt. It does this before every purchase in apps made for children 12 and under, like Color Peek. Google Family Link can also ask you to approve each purchase.
  • Time and attention: no notifications, no rewards for playing days in a row, no countdowns and no paid surprise rewards, and the game never moves on to the next picture by itself. Coins come only from finished pictures and cannot be bought. The game has no play-time limit of its own; Google Family Link can set a daily limit.
  • Contact with strangers: no chat and no accounts, and nothing your child makes is shared.

10. Data Retention

Because we do not collect personal information from children in the first place, there is nothing of that kind for us to store or delete. Play Gaps does not operate a server or database of children's personal information. The limited technical data described in Section 4 is processed and retained according to Google's own retention rules, not ours — see Google's Privacy Policy for details, section 8 for Times Tables for Beginners, and section 9 for Color Peek.

11. Your Rights as a Parent or Guardian

You are always welcome to:

  • Ask us to confirm what information (if any) we hold about your child — as described above, we don't ourselves collect your child's name, photos, voice or any personal information; Google's ad software receives the limited technical data listed in section 4 (such as an app identifier and the internet address)
  • Ask a question about how a specific game works, or how it handles ads or purchases
  • Withdraw your child's use of a game at any time, simply by uninstalling it
  • Ask us to correct or delete information, or object to how it is used — and, in the UK, complain to the Information Commissioner's Office at ico.org.uk
  • Raise any concern directly with us

Play Gaps is a small, independently-run studio — when you email us, you're reaching the actual developer, not a support queue.

12. Children's Privacy Outside the United States

Google Play distributes Play Gaps games worldwide, so briefly:

  • European Union (GDPR) and United Kingdom (Children's Code): we apply the same no-profiling, data-minimizing approach described in this policy to every user, everywhere, regardless of location or age. Times Tables for Beginners also follows the UK Information Commissioner's Age Appropriate Design Code (the "Children's Code"), and was checked against its 15 standards before being offered in the UK.
  • India (DPDP Act, 2023): India's specific rules for children's data are being phased in through 2027. Play Gaps already does not collect personal data from any child anywhere, which aligns with the Act's direction ahead of its enforcement date.
  • Brazil (LGPD and ECA Digital): Brazil has a data protection law (the LGPD) and a law that protects children in apps and online (the ECA Digital). For parents in Brazil, section 16 explains Color Peek in Portuguese: what leaves the phone and why, your rights, the Brazilian age rating, the risks and how we reduce them, and how to report a problem. Under the LGPD, Play Gaps is the "controller" of Color Peek's data: the one who decides how it is used and answers for it. The ad data that Google's software receives is used on the basis of our legitimate interest in showing non-personalised ads, which keep the game free (art. 7 IX), with the child's best interest first (art. 14). Google is also a controller of that data, separately from us, under its own privacy policy, and may use and keep it outside Brazil. Parents in Brazil can also complain to the ANPD, Brazil's data protection agency, at gov.br/anpd.

13. Security

The limited technical information described in this policy is processed by Google's advertising and billing infrastructure, which maintains its own industry-standard security safeguards. On our side, Play Gaps does not operate any server or database storing children's information — removing an entire category of risk by design, not by promise.

14. Changes to This Policy

If we make a meaningful change — for instance, if a future game or feature changes what information is processed — we will update the "Last updated" date at the top of this page and post the revised policy here. We recommend checking back occasionally.

16. Brasil: informações para pais e responsáveis (em português)

Atualizado em 29 de outubro de 2026.

Esta seção é para pais e responsáveis no Brasil. Ela fala do Color Peek, um jogo de colorir para crianças pequenas. O jogo é da Play Gaps, um pequeno estúdio independente. No Brasil, duas leis falam sobre dados e segurança de crianças em apps: a LGPD (Lei Geral de Proteção de Dados Pessoais, Lei 13.709/2018) e o ECA Digital (Estatuto Digital da Criança e do Adolescente, Lei 15.211/2025), que protege crianças e adolescentes em apps e na internet. Você pode ler tudo aqui sem instalar o jogo. A área para adultos do jogo está em inglês, por isso alguns nomes aparecem aqui em inglês, entre aspas.

Classificação indicativa no Brasil: não recomendado para menores de 14 anos. Essa é a classificação da ClassInd, o sistema oficial de classificação indicativa do Brasil, do Ministério da Justiça e Segurança Pública. Ela foi dada pelo questionário IARC, que o Google Play usa. Descritores de conteúdo: nenhum. O 14 vem só das compras dentro do jogo: o jogo tem uma única compra, opcional, que só desliga os anúncios. Fora isso, é só um jogo de colorir para crianças pequenas. Se a conta Google da criança for supervisionada pelo Family Link e a classificação máxima liberada for menor que 14 anos, o Google Play não deixa a criança baixar o jogo.

Resumo dos riscos e do que os diminui. Nós olhamos o que pode dar errado para uma criança no Color Peek e o que diminui cada risco. Revisamos isso a cada nova versão do jogo.

  • Anúncios: um anúncio pode não ser adequado para uma criança. Todo pedido de anúncio diz que o usuário é uma criança e pede só anúncios não personalizados, com a classificação G do Google (para todos os públicos). Bloqueamos os temas sensíveis da lista padrão do Google AdMob. Os anúncios aparecem só nas pausas, nunca enquanto a criança colore. Viu um anúncio inadequado? Avise em contact@playgaps.com. Um toque num anúncio pode abrir a Play Store ou um site.
  • Dados: não tem conta, nem nome, nem nada para digitar, e o jogo não usa o ID de publicidade. O que ainda acontece: pelo endereço de internet do celular, o Google consegue saber uma região aproximada, como a cidade, e pode usar essa região para escolher o anúncio. Ele não escolhe o anúncio pelo que a criança fez antes.
  • Compras: uma criança pode tentar comprar. A única compra fica protegida por uma pergunta para adultos (uma soma simples). Uma criança que já sabe somar, ou que chuta, pode acertar. Depois dessa pergunta, o Google Play sempre pede uma confirmação antes da compra (por exemplo, a senha da conta Google, o PIN ou a digital do celular). Ele faz isso em todo jogo feito para crianças de até 12 anos. Com o Google Family Link, você também pode exigir a sua aprovação para cada compra.
  • Tempo e atenção: sem notificações, sem prêmios por jogar vários dias seguidos, sem contagem regressiva, nada que avance sozinho e nenhum prêmio-surpresa pago. As moedas vêm só de desenhos terminados e não são vendidas. O jogo não tem limite de tempo próprio; o Family Link pode pôr um limite por dia.
  • Contato com estranhos: não tem chat nem contas, e nada do que a criança faz é compartilhado.

O que o jogo guarda no celular. Para a criança continuar de onde parou, o jogo guarda no celular: as moedas; os desenhos terminados (as figurinhas), na ordem em que foram terminados, e quais delas a criança já viu no álbum; as estrelas de ouro; quanto da vila já ganhou cor; até quando vale o desbloqueio de 24 horas de um grupo de desenhos especiais; se "Turn Ads Off" foi comprado; se o jogo já foi aberto antes; o que o menu já mostrou, para nada se repetir; e o último dia em que a animação de abertura passou inteira. Não tem nada para digitar, nem cadastro, nem conta. O jogo não envia nada disso para nós nem para ninguém. Se o backup do celular estiver ligado, o Android pode copiar esse progresso para a conta Google usada no backup, para ele voltar num celular novo. Ele também pode passar direto para um celular novo quando você troca de celular. Nós não vemos esses backups.

O que sai do celular, e por quê. O jogo não pede acesso à câmera, ao microfone, aos contatos nem à localização. O código do jogo não abre conexões próprias com a internet e não usa ferramentas de análise, como Firebase ou Unity Analytics. Sem você fazer nada, dados saem do celular por estes programas, que funcionam dentro do jogo. Fora isso, só sai o que um adulto escolhe abrir ou enviar (último item da lista):

  • Anúncios (Google AdMob). Os anúncios mantêm o jogo grátis. Até a compra de "Turn Ads Off", o programa de anúncios do Google é ligado cada vez que o jogo abre (mesmo na primeira vez, quando ele ainda não mostra anúncios). O Google recebe: o endereço de internet do celular (endereço IP), que mostra uma região aproximada, como a cidade, nunca o lugar exato; como o jogo e os anúncios foram usados (a abertura do jogo, toques em um anúncio, um anúncio visto); como o jogo e o programa de anúncios estão funcionando, por exemplo o tempo que o jogo leva para abrir; e identificadores do aparelho e da conta, como o "app set ID" do Google e, se houver uma conta Google no celular, identificadores ligados a ela (nunca o ID de publicidade). Tudo é enviado de forma protegida (criptografada). Serve só para mostrar e contar anúncios, evitar que o mesmo anúncio apareça demais, impedir fraudes e manter os anúncios funcionando. O Google diz que apaga parte do endereço de internet dos registros de anúncios depois de 9 meses, e os identificadores depois de 18 meses (Google: como guardamos os dados).
  • Ferramenta de consentimento do Google. Até a compra de "Turn Ads Off", cada vez que o jogo abre, em qualquer país, e antes de ligar os anúncios, o jogo pergunta a essa ferramenta o que a lei permite no lugar onde o celular está. O jogo avisa que todo usuário está abaixo da idade de consentimento, então a criança nunca é chamada a aceitar nada. Como toda conexão pela internet, isso mostra ao Google o endereço de internet do celular.
  • Pagamentos (Google Play Billing e Unity In-App Purchasing). Até a compra de "Turn Ads Off" ser feita ou restaurada neste celular, o programa de compras da Unity, que funciona dentro do jogo, se conecta ao Google Play cada vez que o jogo abre. Ele pede o preço e confere se a compra já foi feita antes. Nessa hora, o programa de pagamentos do Google Play envia ao Google registros técnicos sobre o próprio funcionamento: se as chamadas ao Google Play deram certo, as versões do jogo e do programa, o modelo e as configurações do celular (como idioma, país e tipo de conexão) e o endereço de internet. O Google usa isso para as compras funcionarem. Depois da compra, nas próximas vezes que o jogo abrir, ele não se conecta mais ao sistema de pagamentos. O jogo nunca liga os serviços on-line da Unity, que mandariam à Unity dados técnicos sobre o programa de compras.
  • Play Store e compartilhar. Só depois de responder à pergunta para adultos, um adulto pode: abrir a página do jogo na Play Store para dar uma nota; abrir a página dos nossos outros jogos; abrir esta política no navegador; ou compartilhar o jogo. O compartilhamento manda só uma mensagem pronta, com o nome do jogo e o link da Play Store, e nada sobre a criança ou o celular. O que você faz na Play Store fica entre você e o Google e segue a Política de Privacidade do Google.

Importante. Enquanto os anúncios estão ligados (até a compra de "Turn Ads Off"), esses dados técnicos vão para o Google cada vez que o jogo abre. Não dá para usar a versão grátis sem isso. Se você não quiser, pode desinstalar o jogo ou comprar "Turn Ads Off". Depois da compra, o jogo não pede mais anúncios. Para usar os seus direitos sobre esses dados, veja "Seus direitos", abaixo.

Anúncios: feitos para crianças, não personalizados e sem perfil.

  • Os anúncios vêm só do Google AdMob, que está na lista do Google Play de serviços de anúncios aprovados para apps de crianças. Nenhuma outra rede de anúncios é usada.
  • Todo pedido de anúncio diz que o usuário é uma criança, abaixo da idade de consentimento, e pede só anúncios não personalizados, com a classificação G do Google (para todos os públicos). Os anúncios nunca são escolhidos pelo comportamento, pelos interesses ou por um perfil da criança. Eles podem ser escolhidos pelo tipo de jogo e pela região aproximada do celular, como a cidade. O jogo não usa o ID de publicidade do celular. Nas configurações de anúncios, bloqueamos os temas sensíveis da lista padrão do Google AdMob.
  • Os anúncios aparecem só nas pausas, nunca enquanto a criança está colorindo. Um anúncio de tela cheia pode aparecer quando a criança termina um desenho e toca no botão para seguir ("Next"), ou logo depois de voltar ao menu. Passam pelo menos 5 minutos entre dois anúncios de tela cheia, e nenhum aparece nos primeiros 5 minutos depois que o jogo abre. Um anúncio pequeno (banner) aparece embaixo da tela só quando um desenho fica pronto, e some quando a criança toca para seguir, para recomeçar o desenho ou para voltar.
  • Na primeira vez que o jogo é aberto depois de instalado, não aparece nenhum anúncio.
  • Depois da compra de "Turn Ads Off", o jogo para de carregar anúncios na hora. Nas próximas vezes que abrir, o jogo não pede mais anúncios e não chama mais o programa de anúncios do Google nem a ferramenta de consentimento.

A compra "Turn Ads Off". É um pagamento único pelo Google Play, não uma assinatura, e só desliga os anúncios. Para chegar até ela, um adulto toca no botão "Parents" do menu e responde a uma pergunta para adultos ("For parents only"): uma soma simples, com três respostas para escolher. O pagamento acontece no Google Play, na tela do próprio Google; nós nunca vemos dados de cartão ou de banco. O Google Play sempre pede uma confirmação antes de qualquer compra em jogos feitos para crianças de até 12 anos, como este (por exemplo, a senha da conta Google, o PIN ou a digital do celular). Com o Google Family Link, você pode exigir que cada compra da criança espere a sua aprovação.

Tempo de tela. O Color Peek não tem uma ferramenta própria para limitar o tempo de jogo. Para pôr um limite de tempo por dia no celular da criança, você pode usar o Google Family Link. O jogo não manda notificações. Ele não tem prêmios diários, nem prêmios por jogar vários dias seguidos, nem contagem regressiva, nem prêmios-surpresa pagos. Nada avança sozinho: o próximo desenho só vem quando a criança toca no botão para seguir. Se ninguém tocar na tela depois que um desenho ficar pronto, esse botão balança a cada 5 segundos, no máximo 3 vezes por desenho (as 2 primeiras com um som). As moedas vêm só de desenhos terminados (5 por desenho) e não são vendidas. Com 100 moedas, a criança abre um grupo de desenhos especiais por 24 horas, sem relógio na tela.

Por que esses dados podem ser usados (base legal). A Play Gaps não guarda nenhum dado sobre a criança em nenhum servidor. Os dados técnicos que o programa de anúncios do Google recebe são usados com base no nosso legítimo interesse de mostrar anúncios não personalizados, que mantêm o jogo grátis (LGPD, art. 7º, IX). Pedimos ao Google o mínimo que conseguimos: anúncios não personalizados, sem o ID de publicidade e sem perfil. O melhor interesse da criança vem primeiro (LGPD, art. 14). A ANPD (Agência Nacional de Proteção de Dados) diz que dados de crianças podem ser usados por outros motivos previstos na lei, e não só com o consentimento dos pais, desde que o melhor interesse da criança venha primeiro (Enunciado CD/ANPD nº 1/2023).
